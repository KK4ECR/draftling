# Being Busy Is Not the Same as Being Productive

### 5-minute talk script

---

Three years ago I looked at my calendar and felt proud of it. Every hour was full. Back to back meetings, calls stacked six deep, my phone buzzing before I even sat down. Someone asked how I was doing and I said "slammed." I said it like a badge.

Then one night I sat down and asked myself a question I'd been avoiding. What did I actually finish this year?

I couldn't answer it. Not in ten seconds, not in ten minutes. A full year of full calendars, and I couldn't name five things that mattered.

That's the night I learned the difference between being busy and being productive. And I've watched that same gap eat away at good, hardworking people ever since.

Here's the difference in one sentence. Busy measures motion. Productive measures progress.

Motion feels good. Motion looks good on a calendar. Motion gets you a compliment at church or the office: "I don't know how you do it all." But motion doesn't ask where you're going. It just asks that you keep moving.

Progress asks a harder question. Did this get you closer to the life you actually want? Not the life you're performing for other people. The one you'd choose if nobody was watching.

I missed my daughter's recital that same year. Not because someone forced me to. I chose a status meeting that could have been an email, because it was on the calendar first and I didn't stop to ask if it mattered more than she did. That's what busy does. It doesn't announce itself as a bad choice. It hides behind a full schedule and calls itself responsibility.

Here's a fact that never changes, no matter who you are. You get 168 hours this week. That's the whole budget. Same for you, same for me, same for the billionaire and the guy bagging your groceries. Nobody gets more. Being busy means you fill all 168 hours with motion. Being productive means you spend a portion of those hours, on purpose, on what actually moves your life forward.

So how do you know which one you're doing? Here's a tool you can use tonight, for free, no calendar required.

At the end of each day, ask yourself one question. What did I finish today that mattered?

Not what did I attend. Not what did I respond to. What did I finish that mattered.

If the answer comes fast, you had a productive day, even if it was quiet. If you have to search for an answer, if you're scrolling back through meetings trying to find something to point to, you had a busy day. You were in motion. You weren't making progress.

Do this for one week. Just ask the question, out loud if you have to, every night before bed. You don't need a new app. You don't need a new planner. You need seven honest answers.

Some nights the answer will be small. "I had a real conversation with my son instead of half listening while I checked email." That counts. Small and true beats big and empty every time.

Some nights you won't have an answer, and that's going to sting. Good. Let it sting. That sting is the most useful feedback you'll get all week, and it's completely free.

I'm not up here to tell you to work less. I'm not up here to tell you to work more. I'm here to tell you that a full calendar is not the same as a full life, and most of us have been grading ourselves on the wrong test for years.

You have 168 hours this week. Not one hour more.

The question isn't whether you'll fill them. You will. Everybody does.

The question is whether, at the end of the week, you can name five things that mattered.

There was a time when I couldn't. I can now. Ask yourself today, and find out which one you are.
